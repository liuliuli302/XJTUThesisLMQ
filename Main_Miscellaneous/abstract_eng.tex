\begin{englishabstract}
With the continuous growth of online video data, automatically extracting semantically complete and narratively coherent key content from long videos has become an important research problem in video understanding. This task faces two core challenges: first, unsupervised methods based on low-level visual features struggle to bridge the gap between visual representations and high-level semantics, exhibiting inherent limitations in topic characterization and narrative preservation; second, a single scoring model cannot simultaneously accommodate multi-dimensional evaluation demands such as narrative progression, visual saliency, emotional expression, and information density, while also lacking adaptive evaluation strategies for different video types. Moreover, supervised learning-based methods rely heavily on large-scale human annotations, which are inherently subjective and further constrain model generalization in open-domain scenarios. To address these issues, this thesis investigates training-free video summarization along two complementary technical paths---cross-modal semantic alignment and multi-dimensional collaborative reasoning---and develops a video summarization framework driven by large language models. The former focuses on bridging the gap between visual representations and semantic understanding through cross-modal semantic alignment, while the latter focuses on improving the comprehensiveness and strategic adaptability of importance assessment through multi-role structured reasoning.

To address the inadequacy of visual features in characterizing video thematic semantics, a training-free video summarization method based on hierarchical semantic modeling is first proposed. The method structures videos into temporal segments, generates frame-level visual descriptions, and progressively aggregates them into scene-level and video-level textual representations, mapping video content into a unified textual semantic space. The zero-shot reasoning capability of large language models is then leveraged for global theme extraction and multi-granularity semantic relevance modeling, combining global thematic constraints with local visual evidence for frame-level importance estimation, thereby transforming video summarization from visual representativeness selection into a problem of semantic understanding and matching. Furthermore, to address the limited expressiveness of a single scoring model under multi-dimensional evaluation objectives, a multi-agent collaborative reasoning framework for video summarization is proposed. The framework decomposes unified single-model scoring into a structured reasoning process of global planning and multi-role cooperation, where a Planner agent performs video theme inference and dynamic expert weight allocation, and Story, Visual, Emotion, and Information expert agents collaboratively score segments from complementary dimensions. A lightweight memory mechanism is incorporated to enhance temporal coherence, and segment-level scores are mapped to frame-level summarization results through segment-to-frame mapping and normalization.

Experiments on the SumMe and TVSum datasets demonstrate that both proposed methods achieve stable and competitive summarization performance without any task-specific training. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of key components including hierarchical caption aggregation, semantic scoring, multi-expert collaboration, and post-processing strategies.

\end{englishabstract}
