\begin{englishabstract}
With the continuous growth of online video data, automatically extracting semantically complete and narratively coherent key content from long videos has become an important research problem in video understanding. This task faces two core challenges. The first is that unsupervised methods built on low-level visual features struggle to bridge the gap between visual representations and high-level semantics, exhibiting inherent limitations in topic characterization and narrative preservation. The second is that a single scoring model is inherently unable to accommodate multi-dimensional evaluation demands---such as narrative progression, visual saliency, emotional expression, and information density---and lacks adaptive evaluation strategies tailored to different video types. Moreover, supervised learning-based methods rely heavily on large-scale human annotations, which are inherently subjective and further constrain model generalization in open-domain scenarios. To address these issues, this thesis investigates training-free video summarization through two complementary technical approaches: cross-modal semantic alignment and multi-dimensional collaborative reasoning, and develops a video summarization framework driven by large language models. The former focuses on bridging the gap between visual representations and semantic understanding through cross-modal semantic alignment, while the latter focuses on improving the comprehensiveness and strategic adaptability of importance assessment through multi-role structured reasoning.

First, to overcome the limitation of low-level visual features in effectively capturing video thematic semantics, this thesis proposes a training-free video summarization method based on hierarchical semantic modeling. The method partitions videos into temporal segments, generates frame-level visual descriptions, and progressively aggregates them into scene-level and video-level textual representations, thereby mapping video content into a unified textual semantic space. Building upon this, the zero-shot reasoning capability of large language models is employed for global theme extraction and multi-granularity semantic relevance modeling. By combining global thematic constraints with local visual evidence for frame-level importance estimation, the proposed approach transforms video summarization from a problem of visual representativeness selection into one of semantic understanding and matching. Furthermore, to tackle the challenge that a single scoring model faces in simultaneously satisfying multi-dimensional evaluation objectives---including narrative progression, visual saliency, emotional expression, and information density---this thesis proposes a multi-agent collaborative reasoning method for video summarization. The framework decomposes unified single-model scoring into a structured reasoning process consisting of global planning and multi-role cooperation: a Planner agent infers the video theme and dynamically allocates expert weights, while four specialized agents (Story, Visual, Emotion, and Information) collaboratively score segments across complementary dimensions. To enhance temporal coherence, a memory mechanism is introduced that captures cross-segment dependencies. Finally, segment-level scores are converted to frame-level summarization results through segment-to-frame mapping and score normalization.

Experiments on the SumMe and TVSum datasets demonstrate that both proposed methods achieve stable and competitive summarization performance without any task-specific training. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of key components including hierarchical caption aggregation, semantic scoring, multi-expert collaboration, and post-processing strategies. Overall, this thesis demonstrates that, by leveraging the semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities of large language models, video summarization can move beyond reliance on low-level visual features and instead accomplish key content selection through semantic modeling and collaborative reasoning, thus offering a viable technical path toward training-free video summarization in open-domain scenarios.
